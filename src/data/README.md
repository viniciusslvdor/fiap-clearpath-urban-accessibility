# ClearPath data: origin, processing and limitations

| File | Origin | Real or simulated? | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reports_train.csv` (1,320 rows) | Generated by `src/backend/scripts/generate_dataset.py` (seed 42) from 72 sentence templates written for this project | **Synthetic** | Classifier training and cross-validation |
| `reports_blind_test.csv` (60 rows) | Written separately, with vocabulary different from the generator's templates; never used in training | **Hand-written** (not reports from real citizens) | Final classifier evaluation |
| `demo_images/calibration/` (45 images) | `generate_demo_images.py`, seed 1 | **Synthetic** | Choosing the damage index thresholds |
| `demo_images/test/` (45 images) | `generate_demo_images.py`, seed 2 | **Synthetic** | Final computer vision evaluation |
| `responsible_agencies.json` | Real São Paulo agencies and real Brazilian regulations (Brazilian Inclusion Law, NBR 9050, NBR 16537, Traffic Code, Law 10,098/2000) | Real agencies, **fictitious e-mails** (`.invalid`) | Automatic routing and deadlines (SLA) |
| `runtime/` (not in Git) | Created by `seed_demo.py` and by the API | **Simulated** (dates and agency responses are randomly drawn) | SQLite database, processed photos, `.eml` letters and robot reports |

The generated files (`reports_train.csv` and `demo_images/`) are **not stored in Git**. The generators use fixed seeds, so `generate_dataset.py` and `generate_demo_images.py` recreate them byte for byte.

## Real data used in the justification (not used by the code)

- IBGE (Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics), 2022 Census, *Persons with Disabilities*: 14.4 million people with disabilities (7.3% of the population aged 2 or older).
- IBGE, 2022 Census, *Urban characteristics of the surroundings of households* (released on April 17, 2025): 84.0% of residents live on streets with sidewalks, but only 18.8% on streets with obstacle-free sidewalks, and 68.8% live on streets without wheelchair ramps.

## Why synthetic data

1. We did not find a public, labeled dataset of accessibility complaints with a clear license that includes the citizen's free text (which is exactly what the NLP needs).
2. Real sidewalk photos would contain faces, license plates and addresses (personal data) and would require manual labeling.
3. With generated data we know the correct label exactly, and we can insert fake personal data on purpose to test the anonymization.

## Limitations and precautions

- **Generator bias:** the cross-validation F1 is ~1.00 because training and validation come from the same sentence templates. The blind test (F1 = 0.90) is the number that matters. Words such as "number" appear very often because of the generator.
- **Images that are too clean:** real photos have shadows, leaves, oil stains and Portuguese cobblestones, which can cause false positives.
- **Fake personal data:** the CPFs (Brazilian taxpayer IDs), phone numbers and e-mails in the synthetic reports are random (`example.com` domain) and belong to nobody.
- **Next step for real use:** collect real reports with consent in a pilot, label a sample and retrain, measuring again on the blind test.
